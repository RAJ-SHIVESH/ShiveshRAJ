PROJECT 01 / FP&A
GUESTREADY · OPEX FORECASTING

OPEX Forecasting & Variance Analysis.

Monthly OPEX forecasting for the France business, combining historical actuals, supplier assumptions and operational inputs to build a forward-looking cost view and explain movements between forecast and actual performance.

€125K+monthly OPEX scope
20% → 5%forecast variance improvement
10+French cost centres
FP&Aforecasting & variance analysis
01 / CHALLENGE
THE BUSINESS CHALLENGE

The forecast had to be explainable, not just calculated.

Finance needed a reliable view of expected monthly operating expenditure and a consistent way to understand why forecast and actual costs diverged. The objective was to turn recurring variance patterns into feedback that improved the next forecasting cycle.

FINANCE NEEDA forward-looking OPEX view for management.
CORE PROBLEMIdentify where costs diverged and why.
GOALUse actual performance to improve future forecasts.
02 / APPROACH
MY APPROACH

From financial inputs to an explainable forecast.

STRUCTURE INPUTS→BUILD FORECAST→VALIDATE ASSUMPTIONS→COMPARE ACTUALS→INVESTIGATE VARIANCES→EXPLAIN & IMPROVE

Historical actuals, supplier assumptions and operational information were organised into a consistent monthly view. Material forecast-to-actual movements were then traced back to the relevant supplier, operational driver or forecasting assumption before being translated into clear finance commentary.

03 / LOGIC & CONTROLS
FINANCE LOGIC

What caused the movement?

The analysis focused on understanding the reason behind a variance rather than simply reporting the percentage difference.

Variance = Actual − Forecast   |   Variance % = (Actual − Forecast) ÷ Forecast
SUPPLIER VALIDATIONCheck tariffs and assumptions against available evidence.
FORECAST VS ACTUALMake the size and direction of movements visible.
ROOT-CAUSE REVIEWConnect unusual variances to a credible underlying driver.
04 / RESULTS
RESULTS & IMPACT

A tighter forecast-to-actual feedback loop.

The work strengthened the connection between forecast assumptions and actual performance, improving visibility over OPEX movements and supporting more disciplined forecasting.

20% → 5%forecast variance improvement
10+cost centres reviewed

PROJECT 02 / FINANCE OPERATIONS
GUESTREADY · MONTH-END CLOSE SUPPORT

Month-End Accrual & Close Support.

Finance-operations support for costs incurred but not yet fully reflected through supplier invoices, covering unresolved transactions, period review, allocation, reconciliation and subsequent invoice checks.

€10K-€15Kcosts reconciled
€3K-€5Kunbooked costs flagged monthly
45+flats covered
FIN OPSmonth-end close support
01 / CHALLENGE
THE BUSINESS CHALLENGE

The invoice date is not always the service date.

At month-end, unresolved transactions required evidence and review so costs could be assessed against the period in which the underlying service was incurred. The work supported the company’s accrual and reconciliation process within its existing finance controls.

FINANCE NEEDSupport appropriate period treatment.
CORE PROBLEMUnresolved transactions needed evidence and allocation.
WHY IT MATTEREDReliable reconciliation supports a controlled close.
02 / APPROACH
MY APPROACH

Trace the transaction before treating it as a close item.

REVIEW DATA→MATCH EVIDENCE→INVESTIGATE→SUPPORT PERIOD & ALLOCATION→RECONCILE→CHECK SUBSEQUENT INVOICE

Relevant Spendesk transactions were reviewed systematically in Excel, matched with supplier and operational information, and investigated where additional context was needed. The resulting evidence supported period review, allocation, reconciliation and subsequent invoice validation.

03 / LOGIC & CONTROLS
FINANCE LOGIC

Recognise the timing of the service, not only the invoice.

An accrual becomes relevant when a service or cost has been incurred but the related invoice has not yet been received or processed. The key was to understand the service period and support the estimate using available evidence.

Accrual = Estimated cost for services received but not yet invoiced
PERIOD CHECKReview when the underlying service or cost was incurred.
ALLOCATION CHECKSupport the relevant GL, cost-centre or operational allocation.
SUBSEQUENT INVOICE CHECKUse later invoice information as further validation.
04 / RESULTS
RESULTS & IMPACT

Cleaner month-end support through reconciliation.

The work created a clearer connection between original transactions, supporting evidence, allocation and subsequent supplier information, improving visibility over unresolved costs.
